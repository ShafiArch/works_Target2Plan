Target2Plan

Ask for the views and the quiet first, then get the floor plan that reaches them.

Target2Plan goes from a room program and two plan-level targets, the Quality View Coverage Index (QVCI) and the Acoustic Quality Index (AQI), to zone assignments, connectivity graphs and schematic floor plans. Two trained models are at its core, a target-conditioned graph network that places the rooms and a connectivity model that links them; a deterministic synthesis turns the result into walls, doors, windows and furniture.

01Targets

Performance as the brief

Two numbers the plan has to reach

Apartment plans are usually judged after they are drawn: once the rooms are placed, someone checks the views and the noise. Target2Plan turns that order around. The designer states the room program, the site (what each façade looks at and how loud it is outside) and two targets, and the plan is generated to reach them.

QVCI weighs each habitable room's view by its share of the floor: the best façade of its zone (vegetation 1.0, sky 0.9, urban 0.7, balcony 0.4, no view 0) times its window ratio. AQI does the same for quiet: the indoor level from the façade noise through walls and windows, against the guideline of the room (30 dB(A) for a bedroom up to 45 for a bathroom). Every request is shown with the range the plan and site can actually reach.

02Zones

Reading real plans

Nine zones, decided by what each room is exposed to

Every room of a real plan is read with probes 0.15 m beyond its walls. A side is exposed when enough of it opens to the exterior, or to a balcony, or carries a window; the exposed sides then decide its zone in a 3 × 3 grid of corners, edges and the interior.

A capacity rule keeps the arrangements realistic: at most two rooms in a corner (three in large plans), three on an edge, one in the interior, and the interior for bathrooms only.

03Learning

How it learns

A corpus of arrangements, and a decoder that listens to the targets

The corpus starts from real apartment plans. Each plan is labelled with the exposure rule, placed on three drawn sites with different views and outdoor noise, and grown into chains of alternative arrangements, until every reachable combination of QVCI and AQI on that site holds at least two examples.

The zone model, cGAT-AR, reads the rooms and the site through a graph attention encoder; a recurrent decoder then assigns one room at a time, with the two targets entering at every step and a capacity mask keeping every arrangement admissible. 793,609 parameters, trained with teacher forcing.

04Graph to plan

From graph to plan

Which rooms open onto which, then walls, doors and windows

A second network predicts the access graph room pair by room pair, and the secondary rooms move next to the room they open onto. The synthesis is not learned: rooms become rectangles of their prescribed area, anchored at the centre of their zone, pushed apart until they no longer overlap, pulled together along the access edges and snapped to a common grid.

05Results

Does it hit the target?

1,673 unseen plans, three sites each, 45,171 requests

The models were tested on plans they never saw, with requests spread over the reachable range of each plan and site. Asked for a QVCI, the greedy decode lands on average 0.027 away, with a slope of 1.01; for AQI, 0.031 and 0.98, on the sites where the acoustics can change at all.

Taking the closest of 12 samples, both targets fall within ±0.10 for 88 to 100% of the requests in every cell of a 3 × 3 request grid. Each target also moves on its own: asked to go from low to high, the plans cover 82% of the reachable QVCI width and 79% of the AQI width.

06Web app

Runs in the browser

No server, no install, nothing leaves the machine

The trained networks were exported to static files and ported to JavaScript; they run in a Web Worker, and on the same inputs agree with PyTorch to about 1e-5. The app follows the desktop workflow, one card per stage, and exports plans, contact sheets, the Pareto front and the synthesis as GIF or MP4.
